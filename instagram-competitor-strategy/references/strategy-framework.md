# Strategy framework

Read this reference when synthesizing supplied competitor and client reports.

## Evidence matrix fields

Use one row per source Reel or report-backed pattern where possible:

Source type, Competitor/client, Handle, Reel URL, Report date, Published date, Sample window, Views/plays, Engagement metrics, Metric definition, Tier/band, Format, Duration, Topic, Audience, CTA, Sentiment, Hook type, Visual hook, Verbal hook, Written hook, Direct observation, Evidence status, Source limitation, Pattern ID.

Keep direct observations factual and concise. Keep performance explanations explicitly inferential. Preserve Unavailable rather than filling gaps with estimates.

## Pattern classification

For each candidate pattern, record Pattern ID, plain-language pattern name, observed evidence, performance signal, competitors observed, client fit, adaptation, Do not copy constraint, test metric, and evidence limitation.

Evidence ladder:

- Strong: comparable visible metrics, clear source rows, and repeated support across at least two qualifying Reels or a clearly exceptional within-sample result.
- Moderate: one qualifying Reel plus a coherent repeated qualitative feature, or repeated rows with incomplete metrics.
- Hypothesis: one example, thin sample, incomparable metrics, or an interpretation that needs testing.
- Unavailable: no usable public metric or insufficient source detail.

## Recommendation prioritization

Prioritize using evidence strength, client fit, learning value, and production effort. Do not create a numerical score or imply mathematical precision when reports do not support it. A recommendation is ready for the plan only when it has a source, adaptation, CTA, test metric, and constraint.

## Eight-week plan shape

- Weeks 1-2: baseline and rapid tests; profile and CTA check, two or three controlled creative tests, baseline metrics.
- Weeks 3-4: repeat early winners; variations of strongest format and hook; remove weak assumptions.
- Weeks 5-6: build authority and qualification; case studies, proof, objections, process, and audience-specific CTAs.
- Week 7: package the best system; repeat winning combinations with series naming and production workflow.
- Week 8: review and decide; compare like-for-like metrics and decide whether organic and inquiry tracking are ready for paid tests.

Change the sequence when objective or capacity requires it. Every week names the test, audience or problem, format, hook mechanism, CTA, and checkpoint. A content calendar is not evidence of a strategy unless each item links to a pattern or learning goal.

## Measurement rules

Separate reach; engagement; intent such as profile visits, link taps, DMs, and inquiry starts; and business outcomes such as qualified inquiries, booked calls, proposals, and clients. Compare only like-for-like formats and time windows. Note when shares, saves, watch time, reach, or inquiry data are unavailable. Public metrics show content response, not guaranteed client quality or revenue.
